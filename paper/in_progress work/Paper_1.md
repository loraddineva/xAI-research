## Abstract

*(Target: 4–6 sentences. State the problem, method, findings, and contribution. No hedging; no teasers.)*

**Draft:**
LLMs are increasingly used to translate SHAP feature-attribution values into natural-language explanations for tabular model predictions, yet the faithfulness of these narratives to the underlying attribution values has not been systematically measured. This paper introduces a four-type taxonomy of faithfulness failure — sign inversion, rank swap, feature fabrication, and omission — and an automated evaluation pipeline that uses an LLM extraction model to parse narrative claims, then compares the structured output to ground-truth SHAP values. We apply the pipeline to approximately 600 narratives generated by crossing three LLMs (Claude Opus 4.6, Llama 3 70B, Mistral Small), two prompt strategies (Martens-style direct narrative and chain-of-thought), and 100 instances from the Adult Income benchmark dataset (OpenXAI). [Main finding goes here once results are available, e.g.: Chain-of-thought prompting reduces overall hallucination rate by X percentage points relative to the Martens baseline, but does not eliminate rank swap or omission errors; Llama 3 70B produces the highest rate of feature fabrication.] These results establish a baseline for faithfulness measurement in narrative XAI and provide a reusable evaluation framework for future work on LLM-generated explanations.

## 2. Related Work

*(Target: ~400 words. Three subsections. Group thematically. Describe findings, not just topics. Identify what this paper adds.)*

### 2.1 Hallucination in large language models

- Define hallucination as output inconsistent with a verifiable grounding source; distinguish factual hallucination (world-knowledge errors) from faithfulness hallucination (inconsistency with a supplied input).
- Key references: Maynez et al. (2020) on abstractive summarisation; Ji et al. (2023) survey; Huang et al. (2023) on mitigation.
- **This paper's position:** the SHAP-to-narrative task is a pure faithfulness problem — every directional and rank claim maps directly onto a numeric input value with no world-knowledge component.
- **Open question for discussion:** whether observed failures reflect systematic LLM bias toward certain feature types or prompt structures, versus random generation noise.

### 2.2 Explainable AI, SHAP, and LLM-generated narratives

- SHAP (Lundberg & Lee, 2017): dominant post-hoc attribution method for tabular models; game-theoretic grounding.
- Human-interpretability gap: raw SHAP outputs are difficult for non-experts; natural-language summaries are increasingly used to bridge it (cite XAI + NLG work).
- OpenXAI benchmark (Agarwal et al., 2022): standardised datasets, pretrained models, and SHAP values — used as the data source here.
- Existing work on LLM-generated explanations for tabular predictions assesses quality via human ratings or downstream metrics, not against ground-truth attribution values.
- **Gap:** no prior study operationalises faithfulness as agreement between stated directional and rank claims and SHAP values; no multi-model empirical study of this kind exists.

### 2.3 Automated faithfulness evaluation and prompt strategies

- Evaluation approaches: NLI-based entailment, reference-based metrics (ROUGE, BERTScore), LLM-as-judge; entailment methods perform poorly on numerical claims (cite).
- Rule-based detection offers transparency and replicability for structured verification tasks where ground truth is numeric.
- Direct instruction and chain-of-thought prompting (Wei et al., 2022); CoT improves reasoning on structured tasks but may surface more intermediate errors before the final narrative.
- **This paper's approach:** an LLM extraction model parses implied feature claims from each narrative; deterministic comparison to SHAP operationalises four faithfulness failure types.

## 3. Experimental Design

*(Target: ~400 words. Describe data, models, and prompts with enough detail for replication. Acknowledge limitations plainly.)*

### 3.1 Dataset

- **Adult Income** (UCI / OpenXAI): binary classification (income >50K); 11 features after dropping the uninformative survey-weight column `fnlwgt`; class distribution [X/Y]; 100 test instances used.
- The dataset is paired with a pretrained logistic regression model from the OpenXAI benchmark; SHAP values are computed per instance using `SHAPExplainerC`.
- Adult Income is a widely used OpenXAI real-world benchmark with mixed numeric and binary-indicator features, making it a representative testbed for faithfulness in income-prediction narratives.
- **Scope note:** An initial design included German Credit (Statlog / OpenXAI) for cross-domain comparison; that dataset and its preparation pipeline are archived in the repository (`archive/german_credit/`) and are not part of the reported study.
- **Limitation:** 100 instances is sufficient for a pilot study but limits the precision of per-model and per-strategy estimates. Confidence intervals should be reported throughout. Generalisation to other tabular domains (e.g. credit risk) remains to be tested.

### 3.2 LLMs

| Claude Opus 4.6 | Anthropic API | 0.0         |

- Temperature set to 0.0 throughout to maximise determinism; discuss implications (models are not fully deterministic at temperature 0 with some providers).
- `max_tokens = 512`; this constrains narrative length and may contribute to omission errors — note as a potential confound.

### 3.3 Prompt strategies

Two strategies are crossed with each model:

- **Martens (direct):** a near-verbatim adaptation of the narrative prompt from Martens et al. (2024). The model receives the prediction task, predicted probability and class, a SHAP table sorted from most positive to most negative, and a direct instruction to write a fluent story focused on the most influential positive and negative features.
- **Chain-of-thought:** the same task context and SHAP table, but the model must first (1) rank the top five features by |SHAP|, (2) list negligible features, (3) verify the top feature and its sign, then (4) write the narrative under a `Narrative:` heading. The evaluation pipeline strips Steps 1–3 and passes only the text under `Narrative:` to the extraction model. CoT runs use `max_tokens = 1024`; Martens runs use `max_tokens = 512`.

Templates are Jinja2 files (`config/prompts/narrative.j2`, `config/prompts/chain_of_thought.j2`) with per-dataset variables for task description and class labels. Full templates reproduced in Appendix A.

### 3.4 Experimental factorial

Total narratives: 3 models × 2 strategies × 100 instances = **600 narratives**.

## 4. Hallucination Taxonomy and Detection

*(Target: ~500 words. Present taxonomy as a table; describe detection logic in prose per type; keep worked examples brief — one sentence each. LLM judge in one sentence at the end.)*

### 4.1 Taxonomy overview

The taxonomy covers four distinct ways a narrative can misrepresent its ground-truth SHAP values. Types are mutually non-exclusive: a single narrative may exhibit more than one. Relative effect *size* (e.g. calling a large SHAP contribution “minor”) is not treated as a separate category; importance ordering is captured by **rank swap**, and direction by **sign inversion**.


| Type                | Definition                                                                    | Detection summary                                        |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sign inversion      | Narrative states the wrong direction of a feature's effect                    | Extraction `sign` vs. sign of SHAP value                 |
| Rank swap           | A feature described as most important is not rank-1 by |SHAP|                 | Extraction `rank` vs. SHAP rank among mentioned features |
| Feature fabrication | Narrative mentions a feature not present in the dataset's feature set         | Non-empty `unknown_features` list in extraction JSON     |
| Omission            | At least one top-*k* feature (default *k* = 3) not mentioned in the narrative | Top-*k* SHAP features absent from extraction `features`  |


### 4.2 Detection procedure

For each narrative, an **extraction model** (second LLM) receives the narrative text, the dataset feature list, and the prediction task description. It returns JSON: for each mentioned feature, `rank`, `sign`, `value`, and `assumption`; plus `unknown_features` for names not in the dataset. A deterministic **comparison step** then checks the extraction against ground-truth SHAP values and sets four binary flags. A narrative is marked `any_hallucination = 1` if at least one type is flagged. Parse failures and ambiguous extractions are logged in a `notes` field for manual review.

The omission rule is parameterised by `top_k_features` in `config/default.yaml` (default *k* = 3). Human-in-the-loop validation against a gold-standard label set is planned as future work.

### 4.3 Extraction robustness check

Hallucination flags depend on the extraction model parsing the narrative correctly. To quantify
extraction reliability independently of narrative faithfulness, we run a **robustness check**
adapted from semantic-uncertainty methods (Kuhn et al., 2023): for each narrative, the same
extraction prompt is submitted five times at temperature 0.9. Each run returns the same JSON
schema as the main evaluation (per-feature `rank`, `sign`, `value`, `assumption`).

For every feature mentioned in a majority of runs, we compute **sign agreement** and **rank
agreement** as the proportion of runs sharing the plurality value (e.g. a 3/2 split yields 0.6).
**Value agreement** is computed only when at least one run extracts a non-null numeric value.
The **narrative reliability score** is the mean of sign and rank agreement across majority-mentioned
features. Narratives scoring below 0.8 are flagged as low-reliability extractions; those with
fewer than three successful parses are marked extraction-unreliable and excluded from agreement
scoring.

In the main analysis, hallucination rates are reported separately for high-reliability
(score ≥ 0.8) and low-reliability extractions. Flags on low-reliability cases are interpreted
with caution — the detection pipeline may be noisy rather than the narrative unfaithful. The
check multiplies extraction API cost by five; we recommend calibrating the score distribution
on a 10% subsample before running on the full corpus (~600 narratives). Implementation:
`src/evaluation/robustness.py`, `scripts/run_robustness.py`.

## 6. Discussion and Conclusion

*(Target: ~350 words. Three subsections: interpretation, limitations, conclusion. No new claims in conclusion. Tie back to opening problem.)*

### 6.1 Interpretation

- [Draft after results are in.]
- Why might omission and rank swap be more prevalent than fabrication? (Feature names that are absent leave no surface for detection; rank errors require the model to track ordering, not just direction.)
- Why might CoT help or not help with specific types?
- What does this imply about the reliability of LLM-generated XAI in practice?
- Whether failures reflect systematic LLM bias toward certain feature types (categorical, sparse, highly correlated) or prompt structures — or are random generation noise — is an open question for future work.

### 6.2 Limitations

- **Detection coverage.** Errors in the extraction model propagate to comparison flags (e.g. missed feature mentions, mis-parsed direction). Precision and recall against a human-labelled gold standard have not been measured. The robustness check (Section 4.3) partially addresses this by flagging narratives where repeated high-temperature extractions disagree; low-reliability flags should not be treated as ground-truth hallucinations without manual review.
- **Dataset and model scope.** One tabular dataset (Adult Income); three LLMs at temperature 0.0. Results may not generalise to other domains (e.g. credit risk), high-dimensional or sparse data, non-linear models (e.g., gradient boosting), other model families, or non-zero temperatures.
- **Sample size.** 100 instances is sufficient to detect large effects; small interaction effects between model and strategy at the per-type level are underpowered.
- `**max_tokens = 512`.** This constraint may inflate omission rates. Results should be interpreted with this confound in mind.
